FCE-site wireframes — task D-001 · M1 checkpoint black & white · no colour · no typeface chosen

Two screens, five layout options each. The options are not five styles of the same layout — they are five different answers to what is the most important thing on this screen, what sits next to it, and what stays out of the way until it is needed. Pick one per screen. Everything downstream is built on what you pick.

Screen 1

The mission screen →

Carries the brief, the objective, the analysis, the run control and its live progress, the histogram, the cutflow, and — from mission 3 — μ and the significance gauge. Plus some sense of the campaign around it.

  1. 1 · Split Bench — build left, results right, newest run replaces the last.
  2. 2 · Notebook Spread — method on the left page, a growing run log on the right. recommended
  3. 3 · Focus Stage — one step of the loop at a time, full width.
  4. 4 · Result First — the plot is the page; the analysis is one line under it.
  5. 5 · Long Column — one scrolling column, sticky run bar at the bottom.

Recommended: 2 · Notebook Spread. It is the only layout where a new run does not destroy the last one, so "change one thing and compare" — the method being taught — is done by the page rather than asked of a 15-year-old's memory. It is also the only one where missing the objective looks like ordinary work rather than an error, and where the lab-notebook direction is load-bearing rather than decorative. It costs the most to build, and at 768 px it stacks into Option 5, so choosing it means building both.

Screen 2

The recipe-card builder →

The vertical pipeline — Data → Filter → Observable → Plot. Addable, removable, reorderable where it means anything, collapsible once configured, gated per mission, and readable top to bottom as a sentence.

  1. 1 · Card Stack — a vertical list of cards, each collapsing to one line. recommended
  2. 2 · Sentence Strip — the analysis written as an English sentence with clickable words.
  3. 3 · Two Pane — a short step list on the left, settings for one step on the right.
  4. 4 · Guided Slots — a printed skeleton of an analysis that you fill in.
  5. 5 · Deck and Tray — click cards out of a tray; missions unlock new ones.

Recommended: 1 · Card Stack, with one change borrowed from Option 2 — a collapsed card renders as a clause of a sentence ("Take data at 91 GeV from the IDEA detector.") rather than as a settings summary. That costs nothing and is the difference between a stack that reads as a sentence, which the brief requires, and one that merely lists what you picked. Option 4 is the close alternative if playtesting shows students floundering in mission 1 — the underlying config is identical, so switching later is front-end work, not a rebuild.

How to read these

  • Open a screen, then use the tabs across the top to move between the five options. The last tab is a Summary with two comparison tables and the recommendation written out.
  • Under each wireframe is a list headed "What this option does, and what it costs". The numbered circles in the wireframe point at the numbered notes.
  • Everything is black and white on purpose. There is no colour, no chosen typeface and no visual system yet — those come next, in D-002, built on whichever layouts you pick. The browser's default font is used deliberately so that nothing here reads as a type decision.
  • Content is realistic, not lorem ipsum: it is mission 1, the Z peak at 91 GeV, with a real cutflow and a real miss.

What was ruled out before drawing anything

  • A drag-and-drop node canvas — rejected in the brief, §4 and §8. Nothing here drags.
  • Phone layouts. The three widths that matter are 1440, 1024 and 768; 768 is the floor.
  • Anything that makes a run feel silent. Every option has a permanently reserved home for the progress bar and its phase label, so the layout never jumps when a run starts.
  • Anything that makes a miss look like an error. No banners, no modal, no shouting — a miss is a margin note in the flow of the page.
