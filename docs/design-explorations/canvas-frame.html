D-020 — the canvas frame

What is above this line is the proposal: the canvas spans the page, the two side panels and the results drawer float over it, and the canvas is an explorable map rather than a fixed 704×512 plate. What follows answers the questions that re-framing forces, and says what it costs.

N13's four open questions

Zoom limits: 50% to 200%

A node card's title is set at --text-sm, 14px. At 50% it renders at 7px: still recognisable as a card of a known colour, which is what a student needs when they are looking for where a node is, but not readable — so 50% is exactly the floor where zooming out stops being navigation and starts being blindness. Below it there is nothing left to see, since the graph is a few dozen cards, not a thousand. The ceiling is 200% because a node card carries a fixed amount of text: past double size it has no more detail to give, so further zoom only costs the student the context of the neighbouring nodes. Both limits are enforced in one place and the buttons disable at them, so the control tells you when you have arrived rather than silently doing nothing.

Pan: bounded, to the surface

Bounded. The surface is a fixed 1800×1200 sheet and panning is the browser's own overflow: auto scrolling of it, so the student cannot pan into empty space and lose the graph. That choice buys three things for free that infinite pan would have made us build: scrollbars, which are the only honest indicator of how much sheet is left; keyboard panning, via the arrow keys on the focused viewport, which N13 requires and which no custom transform-based pan gets without being written; and a trivial answer to "where am I", because position is a scroll offset rather than an unbounded coordinate. The cost is a ceiling on how large a graph can grow, and the answer to that is a larger sheet, not an unbounded one.

A left-drag that starts on a node still moves that node

It must, because that is what it does today and a gesture that changes meaning between builds is worse than either meaning. The rule is decided by where the drag starts, not by a mode: a drag beginning on a node card moves it, a drag beginning on the paper pans. This is the same convention every map-with-objects editor uses, it needs no modifier key — so it survives a trackpad and a tablet, which design-brief.md §4 requires of every gesture — and it is why the node's drag handle keeps its grab cursor while the paper gets its own. Panning and node-moving never both fire, because the pan handler ignores any pointerdown whose target is inside a node card.

One reset affordance, and it is "Fit"

Yes, and one rather than two. "Fit" scales and scrolls so the whole graph is on screen, which is a known state that a "reset to 100%" button is not: after reset at 100% a student whose graph has grown past the viewport is still lost, just lost at a familiar magnification. Fit is also the answer to "I have panned somewhere and I cannot find my nodes", which is the only reason anyone presses a reset button on a map. It is a real <button>, so it is on the tab order, and it is the deterministic state any test or screenshot should return the canvas to before it measures anything.

What the overlay costs

The panels now cover canvas instead of taking width from it, and that is a real trade rather than a free win. At 1440 the palette covers the leftmost 368px and the mission panel the rightmost 320px of a full-width canvas; the middle 752px is always clear. At 1024 the two together cover 624 of 1024, leaving 400px clear. At 768 they cover 384 of 768 — exactly half — and the clear strip is 384px, the same working width the old side-by-side layout gave, except that now the covered half still exists underneath and is one pan away. That is the whole argument for the change: what is behind a panel is recoverable by panning or by collapsing the panel, where width taken away was not recoverable at all.

What a student loses when both panels are open: the two outer thirds of what is on screen, and, specifically, any node they have parked under a panel. Three things keep that from biting. The canvas underneath keeps panning while a panel is open, so a covered node is reachable without touching the panel. Both panels already collapse to a 64px strip with one click, which the user has accepted as the mitigation. And Fit is measured against the viewport, not against the clear strip, so it will place nodes under an open panel — which is the honest behaviour, but it means the collapse chevrons have to stay visible and obvious, never fade out. The results drawer costs less: collapsed it is a bar at the bottom edge, and expanded it covers the lower part of the canvas only while a student is reading a result they just asked for.

The e2e coordinate-click caveat: yes, this changes it, and for the worse

The standing caveat is that at 1024 and 768 .canvas-region's horizontal scroll can put the leftmost node outside the clickable area, so an e2e test clicking by coordinate misses. Under this frame that is no longer an edge case at narrow widths — it is the normal condition at every width, because the canvas surface is deliberately larger than the viewport and the visible window into it now depends on two pieces of state, the scroll offset and the zoom. A coordinate computed from a node's model position is wrong unless both are known.

What the frontend tasks that follow need to do about it: stop clicking by coordinate. Every node is a real element with a stable data-node-id, and Playwright's locator.click() scrolls it into view and hits its centre, which is correct at any zoom and any scroll offset. Where a test genuinely needs a coordinate — a drag, a drop on empty paper — it should press Fit first, read the node's live getBoundingClientRect(), and work from that, never from the surface coordinate. Assertions about position belong in surface units read back from the node, not in client pixels. This is a bigger ask of the e2e suite than the old caveat was, and it is better to raise it now than to discover it as flake.

What was deliberately not built here

No minimap: the sheet is bounded and Fit answers the same question in one button. No zoom-to- selection, no pan inertia, no momentum easing — the design manual rules out springy motion, and inertia on a bounded sheet mostly produces overshoot into an edge. No second gesture for zoom beyond wheel and the buttons; pinch is the browser's own and is not ours to intercept.
